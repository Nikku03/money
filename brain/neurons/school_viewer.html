Simplex School

The Catch circuits wake up in a world with gravity, which their hand-wired straight-line rule never accounted for. One layer of synapses can now change. Each circuit learns from where its own balls land. It can also learn from its peers' serves and copy their knowledge, but one peer is broken and reports every landing mirrored. In the trusted mode, a peer's influence has to be earned by tests against the learner's own experience.

What can change

Each of the 1,168 prediction cells connects to all 8 landing cells. Its eight weights are its expectation of where the ball lands when it fires. The strongest is always 0.2, enough to fire a landing cell alone, and the rest are in proportion. When a ball crosses the paddle line, every prediction cell that fired during the serve moves 30% of the way toward that landing. Everything else stays wired by hand.

Learning from others

After each round of 10 serves, a circuit learns from each peer's serves as if they were its own (at 0.3 × trust) and moves its expectations toward each peer's (at 0.2 × trust). Its circuits are wired alike, so a peer's serve is one it could have seen itself.

Earning trust

Trust is the lower of two scores. One is how well the peer's knowledge predicts my last 40 serves. The other is how often the landings it reports agree with what I would predict for those serves. Both are scaled from 40% (no trust) to 100% (full trust). A source carries weight only through its tested record.
