Simplex Pong

A circuit of Izhikevich neurons plays Pong. Every weight, delay and connection was assigned by hand. Two directed 11-simplices, cliques of twelve neurons wired all-to-all forward, turn a comparison of where the ball is and where the paddle is into a motor command. This is a recorded game, replayed at its real neural speed.

How a frame flows

Every 20 ms an electrode pulse drives the 8 ball cells and 8 paddle cells, and the cells nearest each object fire. Each of the 64 coincidence cells Cij hears one ball band and one paddle band at 0.08 each. Its threshold is near 0.135, so it fires only when both arrive together. Ball above paddle (i > j) feeds the UP clique, below feeds DOWN, and aligned feeds HOLD, which inhibits both.

Why an 11-simplex

In each clique, neuron k hears all k neurons before it, and their weights share 0.3. The delay from j to k is (k−j−1)·2.5 ms + 1 ms, so every predecessor's spike lands on k at the same moment. The volley runs source to sink in about 30 ms. A lone stray spike can't carry it through, and it survives the loss of most single members.
