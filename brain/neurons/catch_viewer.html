Simplex Catch

The ball is fast and bounces off the walls, and the paddle's top speed is half the ball's. Chasing the ball arrives late, so the circuit has to work out where the ball will land and go there early. It measures motion with 120 ms delay lines and predicts with 1,168 cells that each feed one landing spot, worked out by a rule that folds in the bounces. The same two 11-simplex cliques as Simplex Pong drive the paddle.

Seeing motion

A motion cell R(i,s) hears height cell i directly and cell i−s through a 121 ms axon. The two spikes coincide only if the ball sat at band i−s six frames ago and sits at band i now, so R(i,s) means "moving s bands per 6 frames". The delay line does the remembering.

Predicting the landing

A cell P(x,i,s) fires when R(i,s) and distance cell x arrive together (x's axon is delayed 8 ms to match R's response). Its one output goes to the landing cell for y + v·(frames left), folded back at each wall like light between two mirrors. Try brain neurons wiring --game catch P3_9_+2.
