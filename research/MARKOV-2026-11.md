# A Markov chain of earnings-move sizes, tested on last week

Written Nov 2, 2026. **Exploratory**: no test was declared. The chain was learned on 2011-2023 and tested only
on the week of Oct 26-30, 2026; one week is one draw.

## The chain

- **State.** The size of a stock's previous earnings move (its `car_3d` against SPY, unsigned) in six bands:
  under 2%, 2-4%, 4-7%, 7-12%, 12-20%, 20% and over.
- **Learned from.** The 79,247 research-universe releases of 2011-2023 that `brain/moves.py` fits M1 on, each paired
  with the release before it.
- **Prediction.** The next move's distribution is that of all moves that followed the same state: its expected
  size, its chance of beating any size, and its 80% range.
- **Chain 2** adds the stock's usual move (the median of its last eight) to the state, 36 states in all. A state
  seen fewer than 50 times falls back to chain 1.

The transition table, in percent (rows: the previous move; columns: the next):

| Previous \ next | <2% | 2-4% | 4-7% | 7-12% | 12-20% | 20%+ | Reports |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <2% | 39 | 25 | 19 | 11 | 4 | 1 | 25,550 |
| 2-4% | 36 | 25 | 20 | 13 | 5 | 2 | 18,363 |
| 4-7% | 30 | 23 | 22 | 16 | 7 | 2 | 16,045 |
| 7-12% | 24 | 21 | 22 | 19 | 11 | 4 | 11,659 |
| 12-20% | 18 | 17 | 21 | 22 | 16 | 7 | 5,563 |
| 20%+ | 16 | 14 | 18 | 22 | 17 | 13 | 2,067 |

Size persists: a big move makes a big next move more likely. It persists loosely, though: after a 20%+ move,
the next is under 4% almost a third of the time.

## The strategy on last week: 52 reporters with options

These are the Monday and Tuesday reporters of the options study, with quotes of Fri Oct 23. `imp3` is the move
the options priced for the report; the outcome is the three-day move against SPY.

- **The rule.** Sell when a model expects at least 20% less than the price; buy when it expects at least 20% more.
- **"Gain".** The move points by which the seller or buyer was right: price minus move for a sale, move minus price
  for a purchase. It counts no bid-ask spread and no time value.
- **Brier.** Each model's chance of a move beyond the price, scored against what happened (lower is better).

| Model | Brier | Bets | Right | Average gain (points) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No memory (every stock alike) | 0.276 | 47 (43 sell, 4 buy) | 30 | +1.27 |
| Markov chain 1 (last move) | 0.270 | 41 (36 sell, 5 buy) | 25 | +1.24 |
| Markov chain 2 (last + usual move) | 0.258 | 30 (24 sell, 6 buy) | 14 | -0.05 |
| M1 | **0.248** | 26 (22 sell, 4 buy) | 15 | +0.84 |
| Always sell | – | 52 | 34 | +0.74 |
| Always say 50% | 0.250 | – | – | – |

- **The options were dear last week.** 18 of the 52 moved more than priced. Any rule that mostly sold looked good.
  The no-memory rule sold almost everything, because its expected move (5.2%) sat under most prices.
- **Only M1's chances beat a coin flip, and barely** (Brier 0.248 against 0.250). The chains were too sure that
  big priced moves would not happen.
- **Selling loses big when it loses.** The median sale gained 1.7 points; GLXY (+16.6 beyond the price), MIR (+13.5),
  KNSA (+11.5), FFIV (+11.4) and BBAI (+11.2) took most of it back. A spread costing 5-20% of the premium would
  eat most of the average gain.
- **One week cannot rank the models.** 52 names in one week share one market.
