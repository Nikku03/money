## The claims

- **N1-one-frequency** (modadd): Each hidden neuron of a modular-addition network that generalises reads a and b through one frequency: the median share of a neuron's non-constant Fourier power in its top frequency is at least 0.8 in both U and V. Null: each neuron's weights shuffled over the inputs (200 draws).
- **N1-same-frequency** (modadd): A neuron's output weights use the same frequency as its inputs: at least 90% of neurons have one top frequency in U, V and W. Null: W's neurons paired with other neurons' U and V (200 draws).
- **N1-phase-sum** (modadd): A neuron's output phase is the sum of its two input phases (the identity that turns cos(a)cos(b) into cos(a+b)): the mean resultant length of phase_W - phase_U - phase_V is at least 0.9. Null: output phases shuffled among those neurons (200 draws).
- **N1-faithful** (modadd): That description is the computation: with every neuron cut down to its one top-frequency cosine in U, V and W, the network gives its own original answer on at least 95% of all p*p pairs. Null: each neuron cut down to a different, random frequency (200 draws).
- **N1-hidden-circuit** (memorizer): A network that memorised (train accuracy >= 0.99, held-out < 0.5) already holds the general algorithm: cut down to each neuron's top-frequency cosine it is right on at least 95% of all pairs. Null: each neuron cut down to a different, random frequency (200 draws).
- **N1-rebuild** (modadd): A network written from the bank's facts alone (each neuron's recorded frequency, input phases and amplitudes; output weights from the recorded phase law, never copied) is right on at least 99% of all pairs. Null: none (a threshold).
- **N1-transfer** (modadd): The recorded rule alone (one frequency per neuron, the phase law), with no per-neuron facts, builds a network for modulus 29, which nothing was trained on, that is right on at least 99% of its 841 pairs. Null: none (a threshold).
- **N1-chain-pointer** (chain): The chain engine's network carries a chain in the written-down pointer, not in its continuous memory: with the memory replaced by its mean at every step its 16-step accuracy stays >= 0.99, and with the pointer replaced by its mean it falls to <= 0.5. Null: none (a threshold).
- **N1-chain-lookup** (chain): Each step is a lookup of the pointer's row: with the pointer switched to item j's code at step 3 of 6, the network writes table[j] and carries on along the chain from j on >= 95% of questions. Null: binomial against 1/6, chance for a step that ignores the patch.
- **N1-chain-rebuild** (nomemory): What the probes found predicts a simpler network: the chain engine's network rebuilt without its continuous memory (the tree sees only the table and the pointer), trained the same way on 1-3 step questions, is exact: accuracy >= 0.99 at 16 and at 64 steps. Null: none (a threshold).

## The claims

- **helix-fit**: At the chosen layer, the state at the number token is a helix in the number: the basis [n, waves of period 2, 5, 10, 100] predicts the states of numbers held out of the fit with R^2 >= 0.2. Null: the same fit with the numbers shuffled (200 draws) and with 4 other periods (200 sets). Controls: positive planted helix states; negative the untrained step0 checkpoint, same layer.
- **helix-read**: The number is in that helix exactly: projected onto the helix directions of a fit that never saw it, a number's state decodes to that number for >= 90% of numbers. Null: the same with 4 other periods (200 sets). Controls: positive planted helix states; negative the untrained step0 checkpoint, same layer.
- **locate**: The model reads the number from that token at that layer: with the state there replaced by the state another prompt had at its number m, it continues with m + 1 on >= 90% of prompts. Null: binomial against 1/100. Controls: positive none; negative the same patch at the last layer (the number has moved on).
- **helix-steer**: The model uses the helix: moving only the helix part of a prompt's state from its number n to m (from a fit that never saw m) recovers >= half of the logit gap toward m + 1 that the real state of m opens (median over prompts), more than moving along 4 other periods. Null: the same shift along 4 other periods (50 sets). Controls: positive none; negative the same shift along a helix fitted to shuffled numbers.
- **helix-write**: The helix is the whole number: that shift alone makes the model write exactly m + 1 on >= 50% of prompts. (Expected to fail; see the module docstring.) Null: the same shift along 4 other periods (50 sets). Controls: positive none; negative the same shift along a helix fitted to shuffled numbers.

# How a ~30B model handles language

Claims declared before they ran, after looking only at Qwen3-0.6B (`brain/neuro/langclaims.py`, `docs/NEURO.md`). The BH q is shared with every claim above.

## The claims

- **lang-read**: The model gathers the language into the last token: at the chosen layer, the state at the final ':' (the same token in every prompt) names the sentence's language by nearest language mean for at least the threshold share of TEST sentences, which share no content with the TRAIN ones. Layer: the earliest layer with the best SEL accuracy. Null: means from shuffled language labels (200 draws). Negative control: layer 0, the ':' token's own embedding.
- **lang-swap**: That language part is what the model reads when it names the language: replacing it (state - mean of its language + mean of another) makes the model name the other language for at least the threshold share of TEST sentences. Layer: the layer with the best SEL swap rate (earliest on a tie). Null: the same-size step in a random direction (10 draws); p is one-sided binomial against their pooled rate. Negative control: the swap with means from shuffled language labels.
- **lang-shared**: Meaning and language are separate: with its language mean removed, a sentence's state (averaged over its own tokens) is nearest to its own translation, among the TEST sentences of each other language, for at least the threshold share (chance is 1 in 100). Layer: the layer with the best SEL retrieval. Null: translations shuffled (200 draws). Negative control: layer 0, the tokens' own embeddings.
- **lang-speak**: Language can be written in: adding the difference between another language's mean and English's to every position while the model continues an English sentence makes the continuation that language for at least the threshold share of TEST sentences (French, German, Spanish, Russian, Chinese). Layer: the (layer, strength) with the best SEL rate, layers at (0.25, 0.375, 0.5, 0.625, 0.75) of the depth, strengths (1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0). Null: the same-size step in a random direction (5 draws); p is one-sided binomial against their pooled rate. Negative control: the step with means from shuffled language labels.

Shared meaning without removing the language mean: 0.96. Speak: strength x2.0, random directions 0.00, unsteered continuations English 0.98.
