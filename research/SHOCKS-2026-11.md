# Predicting the unexpected: where shocks land, and what follows them

Written Nov 3, 2026. The exploration below used 2011-2023 only and was committed before the declared tests S1a, S2a
and S2b (`docs/SHOCKS.md`) read 2024-2026; their results come first. The exploration on its own counts as no
evidence.

## Results on 2024-2026 (declared, run once; `research/RESULTS.md`)

| Test | What it asked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **S1a** | Does the radar beat volatility alone, with honest chances? | **PASS**: better in 31 of 33 months (p 0.0001, BH q 0.001); AUC 0.905 vs 0.857; each week's top 5% held 52% of the 20%+ sessions (volatility alone: 45%); its chances ran about a fifth low (0.58% predicted vs 0.72% actual; 6.2% vs 7.5% in the top 5%), inside the declared band |
| **S2a** | Does the typical jump without a results report lag its peers over the next month? | **FAIL**: it beat 50.6% of its peers (p 0.58); 423 episodes |
| **S2b** | Does the average one? | **FAIL (controls)**: +6.26 points against its peers, carried by stocks under $5 (+15.2); its placebo reached p < 0.05 too often (19.5% of draws) |

**Reading.**
- **The radar works out of sample.** Where shocks land can be predicted, and better than by volatility alone.
- **The fade after a jump did not repeat.** It held in every year of 2011-2023 and vanished in 2024-2026, when small
  stocks that jumped often kept going. It is not a reliable edge, and the repo does not treat it as one.
- In 2024-2026, 68% of the shock sessions were up.


## The question

The user asked for something that predicts the unexpected: a drug company up 200% on an FDA Fast Track
designation, a stock up 70%, Brazil's market down 20% in a week. The day and the direction of such a move cannot be
predicted from public data, or the price would already have moved. Two other things might be:
- **where and roughly when** shocks land;
- **what follows** one.

## The data

- **Stocks.** The research universe: 1,568 US stocks with prices since 2010 (`us_broad`, `us_small`, `us_micro`).
- **Units.**
  - One row per stock per week, at the week's last close: 1.06 million stock-weeks in 2011-2023.
  - For the after-shock study, every session 20%+ above or below SPY.
- **Shock.** A session whose return beat or trailed SPY's by 20% or more.

How often a stock-week holds a shock:
- **2011-2019 (fit):** 0.73% of stock-weeks. Up and down apart: up 0.51%, down 0.34% (a week can hold both).
- **2020-2023 (tune):** 0.79%; up 0.58%, down 0.30%.
- **By band, 2020-2023:** large and mid caps 0.26%, small caps 0.84%, micro caps 1.88%.
- **Bigger shocks:** 30%+ in 0.28% of stock-weeks, 50%+ in 0.08%.

## Where shocks land: the radar

Fitted on 2011-2019, judged on 2020-2023 (327,588 stock-weeks). The outcome is a 20%+ session in the next five.

| Model | Log loss | AUC | Each week's top 1% held | Top 5% held | Top 10% held |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base rate only | 0.04600 | | | | |
| 60-session volatility | 0.03682 | 0.859 | 21% of shocks | 45% | 59% |
| Volatility history (the baseline) | 0.03581 | 0.864 | 23% | 47% | 61% |
| **Full model (S1)** | **0.03392** | **0.895** | **25%** | **50%** | **65%** |

**Calibration of the full model** (twentieths of predicted chance, predicted against actual):
- the lowest twentieth: 0.01% predicted, 0.01% actual;
- the middle: 0.45% predicted, 0.48% actual;
- the highest: 8.67% predicted, 8.58% actual.

**What drives it.**
- Mostly the volatility history.
- Then low dollar volume, a report expected within eight days (+0.39 per standard deviation) and distance below the
  52-week high.
- The biotech flag adds little once volatility is known.

**Bigger shocks are easier to locate.** The radar refitted for each size:

| Shock size | AUC | Each week's top 1% held | Top 5% held |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30%+ | 0.922 | 35% | 60% |
| 50%+ | 0.954 | 48% | 73% |

**Direction.**
- 70% of the shock weeks went up.
- Up and down shocks fitted separately barely tell one from the other (AUC 0.60). The model locates shocks; it does
  not sign them.

**A dry run of S1a's gate on 2020-2023.**
- The full model beat the baseline's log loss in 47 of 48 months (sign-flip p 0.0001).
- Actual over predicted was 1.01 overall and 0.96 in each week's top 5%.
- By year: 1.24 (2020), 0.80 (2021), 0.88 (2022) and 0.94 (2023).
- The model then declared was refitted on all of 2011-2023: 1,062,903 stock-weeks, shock rate 0.74%.

## What follows a shock

This covers every stock-session 20%+ above or below SPY in 2011-2023, keeping the first of each 30-day cluster. The
outcome is the excess return over the next 21 sessions.

**Raw.**
- **After jumps:** a median of -3.9% (59% negative).
  - It grows with size: -11.8% after 50-100% jumps, -21.4% after 100%+ jumps.
  - Drug and biotech jumps of 50%+ (73 of them): a median of -15.8%, 75% negative.
- **After falls:** a median of +1.6%. Survivorship flatters this bounce, since companies that kept falling into
  delisting are missing.

**Against equally jumpy stocks.**
- **Why match.** Volatile stocks drift down in log terms and move more, so each jump is set against the stocks in its
  60-session volatility decile (read the close before) that did not shock that day.
- **What is reported for each group:**
  - **vs peers**: the mean of each jump's outcome less its peers' mean;
  - **percentile**: the share of peers the jump beat, averaged (0.5 would be no different);
  - **p**: monthly means with a sign-flip test.

| Up-shocks (2011-2023) | Jumps | Outcome vs peers | Percentile (0.5 = no different) | Behind the middle peer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 3,462 | -2.06 points (p 0.0003) | 0.430 (p < 0.0001) | 58% |
| **Without a results report** | **2,802** | **-2.76 (p < 0.0001)** | **0.412 (p < 0.0001)** | **61%** |
| With a results report | 660 | +0.45 (p 0.70) | 0.500 | 48% |
| 50%+ jumps | 349 | -9.72 (p 0.0003) | 0.325 | 67% |
| Drug and biotech | 647 | -4.79 (p 0.0001) | 0.409 | 62% |
| Under $5 | 1,182 | +2.91 (mean; p 0.98) | 0.459 (p 0.0003) | 55% |

**Is it stable?**
- **By half:** 2011-2017 gave -3.04 points (percentile 0.403); 2018-2023 gave -2.43 points (percentile 0.422).
- **Percentile by year:** below 0.5 in all 13 years, from 0.374 to 0.458.
- **Points by year:** negative in 10 of 13 years.

**Reading.**
- **Jumps without news fade; jumps on results do not.** This is the pattern earlier studies found: no-news price
  shocks reverse, while news-backed ones hold.
- **Under $5 the typical jump fades, but the average does not.** A few keep exploding, so betting against all of
  them loses on average. Shorting a stock that just jumped also costs borrow fees and risks a squeeze. None of that is
  measured here.

## Limits

- **Survivorship.** The universe is chosen today. Companies that were bought out or delisted are missing, along with
  their takeover jumps and collapses. This undercounts shocks and flatters what follows a jump; the fade measured here
  is if anything too small.
- **The smallest and newest stocks are missing.** The universe needs prices since 2010, so most of the tiny companies
  that jump 100%+ on a designation are not in it (Supernus, up 49% today, is not either).
- **Industry codes are today's.**
- **This is exploration.** The tests below were declared after it and read only 2024-2026.

## Declared

- **S1a:** the radar beats the volatility-only baseline, calibrated, on 2024-01 to 2026-09.
- **S2a:** the typical up-shock episode without a results report lags its volatility peers over 21 sessions.
- **S2b:** the average one does.

Their results are in `research/RESULTS.md`, PASS or FAIL.
