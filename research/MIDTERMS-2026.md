Second World War, and this year's polls fit that pattern.

## What each side promised

**Republicans (President Trump's closing case).**
- The midterm is a referendum on his presidency, and losing Congress would "lead to very bad things": an undone
  border and deportation policy.
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act's tax cuts, which the Tax Foundation puts at about $3,752 per person in 2026.
- Tariffs, credited with bringing investment home, though some grocery tariffs were rolled back to ease prices.
- A $5,000 "Trump dividend" per adult if Republicans keep both chambers (Dallas convention, Sep 9). No bill
  authorises it; an earlier $2,000 tariff-check promise has not been paid, and fact-checkers rate the funding claim
  false.
- Headwinds in the coverage: the Iran war's effect on gas prices and an approval rating reported in the low 30s
  (one outlet, unverified).

**Democrats (Jeffries, Schumer).**
- One umbrella: affordability, meaning lower costs for health care, housing, food and energy.
- Restore the ACA enhanced premium tax credits, which expired Jan 1, 2026 (average marketplace deductibles rose
  about 37%).
- Use the government's power to negotiate drug prices.
- "End the tariffs", which they call a tax on families.
- "Stop the war."
- Oversight and investigations of the administration.
- Critics note few point-by-point plans.

**Backdrop that limits what can change.**
- The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026 (6-3, Learning Resources v. Trump), and the
  Court of International Trade ordered refunds.
- The administration replaced them with a 10% global tariff under Section 122, with other tariffs under other laws.
- The president keeps his veto, so a Democratic Congress can block, investigate, set spending and (with the Senate)
  confirm or refuse nominees. It cannot repeal his policies on its own.

## How to read it

- **The House is priced.** At 92-95% odds, a Democratic House is not news on Wednesday. What can move prices is a
  surprise, mostly in the Senate (27-35% for Republicans by market and models), and later the policy fights:
  - ACA subsidies in a spending deal;
  - crypto and AI bills;
  - tariff extensions;
  - investigations.
- **Election day is a known catalyst.** Expect bigger-than-usual moves; the Oct 30 VIX close of about 19 priced
  about a 1% S&P move a day.
- **Direction is not something this repo has found predictable.** The election's sector stories are views from
  analysts and two past analogues that disagreed with each other.

**Reading.**
1. **Wednesday, if Democrats win both (the favourite).** Expect modest drops, because the win is mostly priced. The
   options' election share is about ±10% for GEO and CoreCivic, ±9% for Trump Media, ±3% for UnitedHealth and Coinbase,
   and ±1-2% for defense, banks and automakers.
2. **If Republicans keep the Senate (~30%).** The same names would likely rise instead, and by more, since that is the
   surprise.
3. **The bigger moves come later, one fight at a time.** The funding deadline is Dec 11. The new Congress sits in
   January, and the FY2027 budget, DHS money, the war supplemental, the F-35 deal, data-center costs and investigations
   follow. Single headlines moved these groups 7-22% this year, though most came from the administration, not Congress.
